# Bond yields

The yield of each nation's government bonds, by their term to maturity, in percent a year, drawn on the left axis. Bond yields draws them; Yield terms chooses the terms, from 1 month to 30 years: by default the 3-month, 2-, 5-, 10- and 30-year, and for the UK and Germany their own (5, 10 and 20 years; 2, 5, 10 and 30). A term a nation's sources don't have is named in the log and left off its chart. The difference between two terms is drawn by [Yield spreads](?help=spreads), and with [the latest values](?help=latest-values) each line runs on to the day's quotes.

A yield published once a month is drawn as a step held for its month, a daily one as a line. A gap of up to three days in a daily series (a holiday, a missing print) is bridged; a longer one is a break in the line.

## Canada

- **3-month, 1934 to 2000: the treasury-bill auctions.** The Bank of Canada's table of the average yield at the 3-month treasury-bill auction ([its PDF](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2010/09/selected_historical_v122541.pdf)), one value a month: to 1952 the month's average of the tenders, from 1953 to 1960 the tender after the month's last Wednesday. The first public tender was on 1934-03-01.
- **2-year, 1949 to 2000.** From 1982-06 the Bank's table of the 2-year benchmark bond yield ([its PDF](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2010/09/selected_historical_v122538.pdf)), the mid-market closing yield of selected Canada bonds maturing in about two years, monthly. Before it, from 1949, the Bank's average yield of Canada bonds of 1 to 3 years, the last Wednesday of each month, from Statistics Canada's table [10-10-0122](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=1010012201); over 1982 to 2000 that average ran 0.08 points above the benchmark.
- **5-year, 1951 to 2000.** From 1980-11 the Bank's table of the 5-year benchmark bond yield ([its PDF](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2010/09/selected_historical_v122540.pdf)); before it, from 1951, the average yield of Canada bonds of 3 to 5 years from the same table 10-10-0122, which over 1982 to 2000 averaged the same as the benchmark.
- **10-year, 1951 to 2000.** The Bank's table of the average yield of Canada bonds of 5 to 10 years ([its PDF](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2010/09/selected_historical_v122486.pdf)): to 1958 the average of the yields at month ends, from 1959 the yields of the last Wednesday of the month.
- **30-year, 1919 to 2000.** The Bank's table of the average yield of Canada bonds of over 10 years ([its PDF](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2010/09/selected_historical_v122487.pdf)): to 1936 monthly averages of selected long-term issues, from 1937 to 1948 theoretical 15-year yields, from 1949 to 1958 the average of the yields at month ends, from 1959 the yields of the last Wednesday of the month. The table prints 1936 in both of its first two parts; the program takes the first, so the change of series falls between 1936-12 and 1937-01 (3.11 to 2.93).
- **2001 on: the Bank of Canada's daily yields.** Every business day from 2001-01-02: the benchmark bonds' yields for 2, 3, 5, 7 and 10 years, and the long-term benchmark's, drawn as the 30-year ([Selected bond yields](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/interest-rates/canadian-bonds/)); each benchmark is one bond, replaced by a newer one from time to time (the long-term benchmark now matures in 2057). The 1-, 3- and 6-month and 1-year treasury bills' mid-market yields, averages of yields taken through the day ([Selected treasury bill yields](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/interest-rates/t-bill-yields/)). On 2026-10-28 the latest were the 27th's.

The program holds the five tables as transcribed and checked month by month against the PDFs' own text, and the averages of 1 to 3 and 3 to 5 years as read once from table 10-10-0122. The 1-month, 6-month, 1-year, 3-year and 7-year begin in 2001. Canada has no 20-year.

## The United States

- **1962 on: the Treasury's constant-maturity yields.** The Federal Reserve's [H.15](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h15/) release, read from FRED: yields of Treasury securities at a constant maturity, daily, the figures of the Treasury's daily par yield curve. From 1962 the 1-, 5-, 10- and 20-year ([DGS1](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS1), [DGS5](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS5), [DGS10](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS10), [DGS20](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS20)); from 1969-07 the 7-year ([DGS7](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS7)); from 1976-06 the 2-year ([DGS2](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS2)); from 1977-02 the 30-year ([DGS30](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS30)); from 1981-09 the 3- and 6-month ([DGS3MO](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS3MO), [DGS6MO](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS6MO)); from 2001-07 the 1-month ([DGS1MO](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS1MO)). The 20-year has no figures from 1987-01 to 1993-09, a break in its line. FRED had 2026-10-26's yields on 2026-10-27; the Treasury posts each day's the same afternoon, and the latest values take them from there.

The program has no U.S. 3-year.

## The United Kingdom

- **1935 to 1993: the Bank of England's millennium dataset.** "A millennium of macroeconomic data for the UK", version 3.1 ([Research datasets](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/statistics/research-datasets)): the 10-year gilt yield from 1935, a series the dataset splices together, and the 20-year gilt yield from 1963, monthly averages, each until its daily series begins. The dataset ends with 2016 or 2017 and is no longer updated.
- **1993 on: the Bank of England's par yields.** Nominal par yields from the Bank's fitted curve of gilt yields, daily, in its [database](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/boeapps/database/): the 10-year from 1993-11 (IUDMNPY), the 5-year from 1993-12 (IUDSNPY), the 20-year from 2000-01 (IUDLNPY). The Bank aims to publish each day's curves by noon of the following business day ([Yield curves](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/statistics/yield-curves)).

The UK has only these three terms.

## Germany

- **10-year, 1956 to 1972: the yield of public debt securities.** The Bundesbank's monthly average yield of the public debt securities outstanding ([BBK01.WU0004](https://www.bundesbank.de/dynamic/action/en/statistics/time-series-databases/time-series-databases/759784/759784?tsId=BBK01.WU0004)), from 1956-05 to 1972-08, drawn as the 10-year. The series ends in 2020-04. At 1972-09 the fitted 10-year stood 0.18 points above it.
- **1972 on: the Bundesbank's term structure.** Yields of zero-coupon Federal securities, estimated by the Svensson method from the yields of the listed Federal bonds outstanding ([Term structure of interest rates](https://www.bundesbank.de/en/statistics/money-and-capital-markets/interest-rates-and-yields/term-structure-of-interest-rates); the 10-year: [BBSIS.D.I.ZST.ZI.EUR.S1311.B.A604.R10XX.R.A.A._Z._Z.A](https://www.bundesbank.de/dynamic/action/en/statistics/time-series-databases/time-series-databases/759784/759784?tsId=BBSIS.D.I.ZST.ZI.EUR.S1311.B.A604.R10XX.R.A.A._Z._Z.A)). The values at each month's end from 1972-09 for 1, 2, 5, 7 and 10 years, from 1986-06 for 20 years and from 2000-01 for 30, drawn as steps; then daily from 1997-08-07, the 30-year from 2000-08-01. The Bundesbank publishes the day's values during the day: at 11:50 UTC on 2026-10-21 that day's were there.

Germany has no 1-month, 3-month, 6-month or 3-year in the program.

## How the program keeps these figures

Each source's figures are kept in the program as the source published them. A job on GitHub asks every live source each day and adds the new figures; a revision of older ones waits for review before it is taken. When a chart is drawn, the program takes only the newest figures from each source and draws the older ones from what it holds: those from 24 months before the last figure it holds where the source revises its figures (GDP, debt, interest, population and the mortgage rates from surveys), from two months before where it doesn't (the yields, and the rates that are set or traded). If a source doesn't answer, the chart is drawn from what the program holds, with a warning naming the source and its last date. The sources that have stopped (Statistics Canada's old tables, Historical Statistics of Canada, the Bank of Canada's historical tables, the Bank of England's dataset, and the series the Federal Reserve, the Bundesbank and the ECB no longer update) are kept as they were last published, and asked nothing.
