# Mortgage rates

Rates on loans for buying a home, in percent a year, drawn on the left axis as dashed steps: each in the colour of the bond yield of its term where the program has one (the 5-year mortgage as the 5-year yield), the variable rates dash-dotted, the prime rate dotted. Mortgage rates draws them; Mortgage terms chooses the terms: by default the U.S. 30-year, Canada's 5-year, all five of the UK's, the three fixed periods of Germany, France, Italy and the Netherlands, and the variable rate of Portugal and Spain. A term is drawn on its own nation's chart only.

The kind of rate differs from nation to nation, so a level on one chart isn't the same measure as on another: survey averages of the rates offered in the U.S., the major banks' posted rates in Canada, quoted rates in the UK, and averages of the rates on new loans in Germany and the other euro nations.

The prime rate is a term too, for Canada and the U.S.: the base rate banks price variable loans from. Prime margin (points) draws it plus a margin, as in "Prime Rate + 0.50", which the legend shows; a negative margin is allowed. Tracker margin (points) does the same for the UK's tracker mortgage, below.

## Canada

5y, ticked at the start, 3y and 1y: the major chartered banks' posted rates, weekly, the 5-year from 1975 (CMHC's rate from 1951 before it), the 3- and 1-year from 1980. 5y var.: the Bank of Canada's estimated 5-year variable rate, a brokers' average, from 2011-01-06. prime: the chartered banks' prime rate from 1975, drawn plus Prime margin.

- **5-year, 1951 to 1974: the CMHC rate.** Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation's conventional mortgage lending rate for a 5-year term, monthly from 1951, Statistics Canada's table [34-10-0145](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=3410014501). It is drawn only before the posted rate begins, so the program draws it from what it holds, without asking. At the join there is no jump: 11.88 in 1974-12, then 12.00.
- **5-, 3- and 1-year: the posted rates.** The major chartered banks' posted rates for conventional mortgages, weekly (Wednesdays): the 5-year from 1975-01-01 (V80691335), the 3- and 1-year from 1980-01-02 (V80691334, V80691333) ([Interest rates posted for selected products by the major chartered banks](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/banking-and-financial-statistics/posted-interest-rates-offered-by-chartered-banks/)). The Bank of Canada typically publishes them on Wednesdays by 14:30 Eastern time. The legend says "(posted)".
- **5-year variable, from 2011-01-06: the estimated rate.** The Bank of Canada's estimated 5-year variable mortgage rate, weekly (Thursdays), an average of brokers' rates, as the series' code (BROKER_AVERAGE_5YR_VRM) says ([Canadian interest rates and monetary policy variables](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/interest-rates/canadian-interest-rates/)); the legend says "(broker avg.)". It is missing for 28 days from 2013-05-30 and 14 days from 2014-12-04, breaks in the line.
- **Prime, from 1975: the chartered banks' prime rate.** Weekly (Wednesdays, V80691311), from the same page of posted rates. Each bank sets its own prime rate from its cost of funding, which the Bank of Canada's target for the overnight rate influences. It has stood 2.20 above the target on every Wednesday since 2020-03-11, and since 2022 it has moved with each change of the target by the next Wednesday. Valet has a Wednesday's value the day after (2026-10-21's by 12:32 Eastern time on the 22nd). So a change of the target after the prime's last Wednesday, in the Bank's series or announced ([Policy rates](?help=policy-rates)), is drawn on the prime rate from the day it takes effect, 2.20 above the target, named "Announced from" in the legend, until the prime's own series has it; from then the change is drawn from the Wednesday the series dates it by, as every earlier one is. If the prime no longer stands 2.20 above the target on its last Wednesday, nothing is carried, with a warning.

## The United States

30y, ticked at the start, and 15y: Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey on FRED, weekly, the 30-year from 1971-04-02, the 15-year from 1991-08-30. prime: the bank prime loan rate from the Federal Reserve's H.15, daily from 1955-08-04, drawn plus Prime margin.

- **30- and 15-year: Freddie Mac's survey.** The Primary Mortgage Market Survey ([PMMS](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms)), read from FRED: the 30-year fixed rate from 1971-04-02 ([MORTGAGE30US](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/MORTGAGE30US)), the 15-year from 1991-08-30 ([MORTGAGE15US](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/MORTGAGE15US)), weekly. Freddie Mac publishes it each Thursday at noon Eastern time, the average of the rates on loans offered from the Thursday before through Wednesday. Since 2022-11-17 it has been based on the applications lenders across the country submit to Freddie Mac.
- **Prime, from 1955-08-04: the bank prime loan rate.** The rate posted by a majority of the top 25 insured U.S.-chartered commercial banks, daily, from the Federal Reserve's H.15 release ([DPRIME](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DPRIME)).

## The United Kingdom

All five ticked at the start. 2y, 3y, 5y and SVR: the Bank of England's quoted rates, monthly from 1995-01, the SVR carried back to 1939-09 by the Bank's millennium dataset. tracker: Bank Rate plus Tracker margin (1.75 at the start), from 1997-01.

- **2-, 3- and 5-year fixed, and the standard variable rate, from 1995-01.** The Bank of England's quoted rates, monthly weighted averages of the rates offered to households ([Quoted household interest rates](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/statistics/visual-summaries/quoted-household-interest-rates)): fixed for 2, 3 or 5 years on a loan of 75 % of the property's value (IUMBV34, IUMBV37, IUMBV42), and the standard variable rate a fixed rate reverts to (IUMTLMV). Each month is drawn from its first day. On 2026-10-28 the latest month was 2026-09.
- **The standard variable rate, 1939 to 1994: the millennium dataset.** The Bank of England's dataset ([Research datasets](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/statistics/research-datasets)) has a spliced variable mortgage rate, monthly from 1939-09; it carries the standard variable rate back until the quoted rate begins. The line is labelled "Standard Variable Rate (SVR)" throughout.
- **Tracker, from 1997-01-01: Bank Rate plus a margin.** A tracker mortgage follows Bank Rate at a fixed margin, so the program draws Bank Rate ([Policy rates](?help=policy-rates)) plus Tracker margin (points), by default 1.75, from 1997-01, the first month of the Bank's quoted lifetime tracker rate (IUMBV24). That rate stood 1.71 above Bank Rate in its last month, 2025-03. A change of Bank Rate the Bank of England has announced but not yet put in its series is drawn on the tracker from the same day, as on Bank Rate.

## Germany

1–5y, 5–10y and over 10y, all three ticked at the start: the ECB's rates on new loans to households in Germany for house purchase, monthly from 2003-01; before it the Bundesbank's older rates from 1982-06 for the first two, the ECB's estimates from 2000-01 for over 10y. variable, from 2000-01, is offered beside them.

- **Over 1 to 5 and over 5 to 10 years from 1982-06, over 10 years from 2000-01.** From 2003-01, the ECB's bank interest rate statistics for Germany: the rates on new loans to households for house purchase, by the period the rate is fixed for, monthly ([over 1 to 5](https://data.ecb.europa.eu/data/datasets/MIR/MIR.M.DE.B.A2C.I.R.A.2250.EUR.N), [over 5 to 10](https://data.ecb.europa.eu/data/datasets/MIR/MIR.M.DE.B.A2C.O.R.A.2250.EUR.N), [over 10](https://data.ecb.europa.eu/data/datasets/MIR/MIR.M.DE.B.A2C.P.R.A.2250.EUR.N)). The legend says "(new loans, avg.)". Each month is drawn from its first day. On 2026-10-28 the latest month was 2026-08.
- **Before 2003-01.** The ECB flags its figures for 2000-01 to 2002-12 as estimates. For the two shorter periods the program draws instead, from 1982-06 to 2002-12, the Bundesbank's older lending rate statistics: the effective rate on new mortgages fixed for [5 years](https://www.bundesbank.de/dynamic/action/en/statistics/time-series-databases/time-series-databases/759784/759784?tsId=BBIB1.M.DE.B.S.DNB.HYP.F05J.A.E1.11A) as over 1 to 5, and for [10 years](https://www.bundesbank.de/dynamic/action/en/statistics/time-series-databases/time-series-databases/759784/759784?tsId=BBIB1.M.DE.B.S.DNB.HYP.F10J.A.E1.11A) as over 5 to 10, each an average of what the banks reported in the two middle weeks of the month, including the banks of the new Länder from 1991-01. A rate fixed for one term is not a whole period's average and the two statistics were collected differently, so a small step remains at 2003-01: about 0.2 of a point on the first, 0.03 on the second, beyond that month's own fall. Over 10 years has no older figures. It begins with the ECB's estimates, which are the Bundesbank's 10-year rate plus 0.02 in all 36 months, so until 2003 it runs along the line of over 5 to 10.
- **Variable, from 2000-01.** The same statistics' rate on new loans at a variable rate or fixed for up to a year ([variable](https://data.ecb.europa.eu/data/datasets/MIR/MIR.M.DE.B.A2C.F.R.A.2250.EUR.N)), every month there, the ECB's estimates to 2002-12 as for the fixed periods. The legend says "Variable-Rate Mortgage (new loans, avg.)". It is offered beside the fixed periods, not ticked at the start, and drawn dash-dotted in the 1-year yield's colour. On the command line it is `var`: `--ger:mo:var,1-5`.

## Japan

None. Japan's mortgage rates are published by the Japan Housing Finance Agency (its Flat 35 rates), a host the program does not ask; the comparison of mortgage rates leaves Japan off, with a note.

## How the program keeps these figures

Each source's figures are kept in the program as the source published them. A job on GitHub asks every live source each day and adds the new figures; a revision of older ones waits for review before it is taken. When a chart is drawn, the program takes only the newest figures from each source and draws the older ones from what it holds: those from 24 months before the last figure it holds where the source revises its figures (GDP, debt, interest, population and the mortgage rates from surveys), from two months before where it doesn't (the yields, and the rates that are set or traded). If a source doesn't answer, the chart is drawn from what the program holds, with a warning naming the source and its last date. Statistics Canada is checked once a day: on a day it doesn't answer, it is asked nothing more until the next day in Ottawa, and where another source publishes the same figures (the consumer price index at the Bank of Canada, GDP in the IMF's copy at FRED, interest, consolidated and federal, at the IMF itself), that source's newer figures follow Statistics Canada's last, and the warning names them. The sources that have stopped (Statistics Canada's old tables, Historical Statistics of Canada, the Bank of Canada's historical tables, the Bank of England's dataset, and the series the Federal Reserve, the Bundesbank and the ECB no longer update) are kept as they were last published, and asked nothing.
