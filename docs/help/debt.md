# Aggregate public debt

The debt of each nation's governments at each date, on the right axis, in its own currency (as a share of GDP under [As % of GDP](?help=percent-of-gdp), per person under [Per capita](?help=per-capita)). Debt is a stock: each figure is the level on the last day of its month, quarter or year, and the line holds it until the next figure, as a step. A figure is carried at most a year past the last one, so the line stops where a source stops. With levels of government ticked, each level is drawn instead of the total: [By level](?help=levels).

The four nations' figures aren't one measure. Canada's, the UK's and Germany's are general government gross debt as their national accounts define it, with what one government owes another netted out (in Canada's, for debt securities only). The U.S. line adds the federal government's total public debt to state and local debt, with nothing netted out. The older figures before them are narrower measures, bonds and bills alone or one government's debt alone; each nation's section below says which.

## Canada

- **1867 to 1932: federal debt alone.** Historical Statistics of Canada, second edition, series H37 and H38: the federal government's bonded debt less its sinking funds, and its treasury bills, at each fiscal year's end ([section H](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-516-x/sectionh/4057752-eng.htm)). No provincial or local figures exist before 1933, and federal debt was about half the total then, so it is drawn as a curve of its own, dotted, "Federal CDN Public Debt (pre-1933)", and not joined to the total.
- **1933 to 1960: all governments.** The same source: federal H37 and H38, provincial H384 and H385 (net funded debt and treasury bills) and local H400 (net funded debt), added together, for 1933, 1937, 1939, 1941 and 1943, then each year from 1945.
- **1961 to 1989: the national balance sheets.** Statistics Canada's tables [36-10-0533](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=3610053301) (federal government) and [36-10-0534](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=3610053401) (provincial and local governments), annual, 1961 to 2011, no longer updated: the short-term paper and bonds the governments owe, at book value, at each year's end. The tables list short-term paper and bonds twice, once among the governments' assets and once among their liabilities; the program reads the liabilities.
- **1990 on: general government gross debt.** Statistics Canada's table [36-10-0467](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=3610046701), quarterly, its line "Debt": besides bonds and bills it counts loans, pension liabilities and accounts payable. The table notes that it is consolidated for debt securities only. Published about ten weeks after each quarter: the release of 2026-09-11 brought the second quarter of 2026.

Where two sources overlap, the newer one is drawn and the older one is scaled to meet it: by the median ratio of the two over the years both cover (to 1994), reached year by year over the older one's last five years, so that the line has no jump at the join. Historical Statistics' figures for 1956 to 1960 are scaled up by as much as 5.1 % to meet the balance sheets, and the balance sheets' for 1985 to 1989 by as much as 27 % to meet table 36-10-0467, whose debt counts more kinds of liability.

## The United States

- **Federal: total public debt.** The U.S. Treasury's quarterly figure, [GFDEBTN on FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/GFDEBTN), millions of dollars at each quarter's end, from 1966; FRED had the second quarter of 2026 on 2026-10-01. It includes the intragovernmental holdings: the debt the Treasury owes the government's own trust funds, Social Security's among them, as well as the debt held by the public ([the Treasury's definition](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/datasets/debt-to-the-penny/)).
- **State and local: debt securities and loans.** The Federal Reserve's Financial Accounts of the United States (Z.1), [SLGSDODNS on FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SLGSDODNS), millions of dollars at each quarter's end, from 1945; published about ten weeks after each quarter (the next on 2026-12-10). FRED notes that each release may revise the series.

The line is their sum, from 1966, where the federal series begins. With Intraday quotes on end date ticked (`--cur`), federal debt is carried on from its last quarter to the chart's end by the Treasury's daily Debt to the Penny.

## The United Kingdom

- **Gross debt, from March 1975.** General government consolidated gross debt, the Maastricht measure, [BKPX at the ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/governmentpublicsectorandtaxes/publicsectorfinance/timeseries/bkpx/pusf), millions of pounds at each month's end, from the public sector finances. Published about three weeks after each month: the release of 2026-10-21 brought September 2026; the next is 2026-11-20.
- **Before 1975: the national debt.** Central government's debt at the end of each financial year (31 March), from 1691, in the Bank of England's dataset "A millennium of macroeconomic data", version 3.1 ([research datasets](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/statistics/research-datasets)). It is a different quantity from gross debt, so it is drawn as a curve of its own, dotted, "UK National Debt (pre-1975)", until gross debt begins. The two are within 3 % where they meet: £52.1 billion and £53.7 billion on 1975-03-31.
- **Net debt, when ticked under Aggregate debts.** Public sector net debt excluding the public sector banks, the UK's headline measure, [HF6W at the ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/governmentpublicsectorandtaxes/publicsectorfinance/timeseries/hf6w/pusf), billions of pounds at each month's end, from March 1975, published with gross debt. It covers the whole public sector, not only general government: what it owes the UK private sector and overseas, less its liquid assets. Of the four nations only the UK has it; gross debt alone is drawn by default.

## Germany

- **1991 to 1999: year ends.** General government consolidated debt, annual, from the ECB's government finance statistics ([the series on the ECB Data Portal](https://data.ecb.europa.eu/data/datasets/GFS/GFS.A.N.DE.W0.S13.S1.C.L.LE.GD.T._Z.XDC._T.F.V.N._T)), at each year's end from 1991.
- **2000 on: quarters.** General government consolidated gross debt, the Maastricht measure, Eurostat's dataset [gov_10q_ggdebt](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/gov_10q_ggdebt/default/table?lang=en), millions of euros at each quarter's end. The nations send it to Eurostat within three months of each quarter's end ([Eurostat's notes](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/cache/metadata/en/gov_10q_ggdebt_esms.htm)); the second quarter of 2026 is the latest. The ECB's year ends equal Eurostat's fourth quarters where both exist, so no scaling is needed.

Figures before 1999 are Deutsche Marks converted to euros at 1.95583, as the ECB and Eurostat publish them. Germany's figures begin with unification; West Germany's, from 1950, are planned.

## How the program keeps these figures

Each source's figures are kept in the program as the source published them. A job on GitHub asks every live source each day and adds the new figures; a revision of older ones waits for review before it is taken. When a chart is drawn, the program asks the source for its figures from 24 months before the last one it holds, since revisions reach that far back, and draws the older ones from what it holds. If the source doesn't answer, the chart is drawn from what the program holds, with a warning naming the source and its last date. The sources that have stopped (Statistics Canada's old tables, Historical Statistics of Canada, the Bank of England's dataset) are kept as they were last published.
