# By level of government

With a level ticked in a nation's panel, its chart draws [debt](?help=debt) and [interest](?help=interest) for each level ticked instead of the nation's total, each level a line of its own. None ticked draws the totals. The levels each nation offers are its own: Canada's federal, provincial, municipal and non-federal (provincial and municipal together); the U.S.' federal, state, local, and state and local together; the UK's central and local; Germany's federal, Länder, local, and Länder and local together.

A level's figures are that government's own. Debt one level owes another is in the borrower's figures, but netted out of the total where the total is consolidated, so the levels need not add up to the total, and usually add up to more. The same holds for interest.

## Canada

Each level is joined from its own sources, oldest first, as the total is; the non-federal level is provincial plus municipal within each source before the joins. The Canada and Quebec Pension Plans, which the national accounts count within general government, are left out: their liabilities are pension entitlements, not borrowing.

- **Debt, 1867 to 1960.** Historical Statistics of Canada, second edition ([section H](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-516-x/sectionh/4057752-eng.htm)): federal bonded debt less sinking funds and treasury bills (H37 and H38) from 1867; provincial net funded debt and treasury bills (H384 and H385) and local net funded debt (H400) from 1933.
- **Debt, 1961 to 1989.** The national balance sheets of the federal government ([36-10-0533](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=3610053301)), the provincial governments ([36-10-0535](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=3610053501)) and the local governments ([36-10-0536](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=3610053601)), annual, archived: the short-term paper and bonds each owes, at year end.
- **Debt, 1990 on.** Statistics Canada's government finance statistics, table [10-10-0015](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=1010001501), quarterly, the balance sheet of each level: all its liabilities except equity (special drawing rights, currency and deposits, debt securities, loans, insurance and pension schemes, other accounts payable). Published about three months after each quarter: the release of 2026-09-25 brought the second quarter of 2026.
- **Interest, 1926 to 1960.** The 1968 national accounts' government revenue and expenditure by level ([36-10-0177](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=3610017701) annual, [36-10-0142](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=3610014201) quarterly from 1951), hospitals counted with the provinces. Archived.
- **Interest, 1961 to 1990.** The sector accounts, table [36-10-0245](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=3610024501), federal, provincial and local, quarterly at annual rates. Archived.
- **Interest, 1990 on.** Table 10-10-0015, each level's expense "Interest", quarterly, not annualised: the twelve months are the sum of four quarters.

At each join the older source is scaled to meet the newer one over its last five years. The balance sheets hold bonds and bills alone, the newer table every liability, so the debts are scaled up most at that join: federal by as much as 36 %, provincial 61 %, municipal 80 %, over 1985 to 1989. Elsewhere the scaling is smaller: Historical Statistics' provincial and municipal debts by 12 % over 1956 to 1960, and interest by 7 % at most. In table 10-10-0015 local interest dips to about $480 million a quarter in 2007, against about $850 million in 2006 and $770 million in 2008; it is drawn as published.

## The United States

The federal level and the state and local level together are quarterly series; the split between state and local comes from yearly figures, applied to the quarters.

- **Federal debt.** The Treasury's total public debt, [GFDEBTN on FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/GFDEBTN), quarterly from 1966, as on the [debt page](?help=debt).
- **State and local debt together.** The Federal Reserve's [SLGSDODNS on FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SLGSDODNS), debt securities and loans, quarterly from 1945.
- **State and local debt apart.** The state governments' share of the two levels' total debt outstanding at each fiscal year's end, from the Census Bureau's counts: its historical database of government finances for fiscal 1902 to 2008 ([historical data](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/gov-finances/data/historical-data.html)) and its Annual Survey of State and Local Government Finances from 2009 ([the survey](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/gov-finances.html)), each year from 1952. The share is drawn straight between one 30 June and the next and applied to SLGSDODNS, so state plus local always equals it. The program's counts run to fiscal 2024; after it the 2024 share is held, with a warning.
- **Federal interest.** The national accounts' federal interest payments, [A091RC1Q027SBEA on FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/A091RC1Q027SBEA), quarterly at annual rates from 1947.
- **State and local interest together.** [B111RC1Q027SBEA on FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/B111RC1Q027SBEA), quarterly at annual rates from 1947.
- **State and local interest apart.** The national accounts' annual interest payments of state governments ([W756RC1A027NBEA](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/W756RC1A027NBEA)) and local governments ([W856RC1A027NBEA](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/W856RC1A027NBEA)), from 1959 to 2025. Each year's shares are applied to its four quarters, so each year's last twelve months equal the published annual figures, and the quarterly movement is kept.

## The United Kingdom

The UK has no level between central and local government: the devolved governments of Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland are inside central government in its accounts. Non-federal, asked of the UK, is its local government.

- **Central government debt.** [BKPW at the ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/governmentpublicsectorandtaxes/publicsectorfinance/timeseries/bkpw/pusf), total central government gross debt, monthly from 1975, millions of pounds, from the public sector finances (the release of 2026-10-21 brought September 2026).
- **Local government debt.** [MDYT at the ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/governmentpublicsectorandtaxes/publicspending/timeseries/mdyt/edp), local government gross debt, quarterly from 1966, from the government finance statistics: the release of 2026-09-22 brought the second quarter of 2026; the next is on 2026-12-22.
- **Central government interest.** [NMFX at the ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/governmentpublicsectorandtaxes/publicsectorfinance/timeseries/nmfx/pusf), "CG: Current expenditure: Net Interest payable", quarterly from 1946.
- **Local government interest.** [NUGW at the ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/governmentpublicsectorandtaxes/publicsectorfinance/timeseries/nugw/pusf), "LG: Current expenditure: Interest", quarterly from 1946.

Interest is summed over four quarters.

## Germany

- **Debt.** Eurostat's dataset [gov_10q_ggdebt](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/gov_10q_ggdebt/default/table?lang=en), gross debt by subsector, quarterly from 2000: the federal government with its special funds (central government, S.1311), the Länder (state government, S.1312) and local government (S.1313). The social security funds (S.1314) are left out. The levels add up to about 1 % more than the total.
- **Interest.** Eurostat's dataset [gov_10q_ggnfa](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/gov_10q_ggnfa/default/table?lang=en), interest payable (D.41) by the same subsectors, quarterly from 2002, summed over four quarters.

## How the program keeps these figures

Each source's figures are kept in the program as the source published them. A job on GitHub asks every live source each day and adds the new figures; a revision of older ones waits for review before it is taken. When a chart is drawn, the program asks the source for its figures from 24 months before the last one it holds, since revisions reach that far back, and draws the older ones from what it holds. If the source doesn't answer, the chart is drawn from what the program holds, with a warning naming the source and its last date. The sources that have stopped (Statistics Canada's old tables, Historical Statistics of Canada, the Bank of England's dataset) are kept as they were last published.
