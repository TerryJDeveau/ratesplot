# Debt and interest by level of government

With a level ticked in a nation's panel, its chart draws [debt](?help=debt) and [interest](?help=interest) for each level ticked instead of the nation's total, each level a line of its own. None ticked draws the totals. The levels each nation offers are its own: Canada's federal, provincial, municipal and non-federal (provincial and municipal together); the U.S.' federal, state, local, and state and local together; the UK's central and local; Germany's federal, Länder, local, and Länder and local together; Spain's central, regional (its autonomous communities), local, and regional and local together; France's, Portugal's, Italy's and the Netherlands' central and local; Japan's central and local, its debt alone (the Bank of Japan's flow of funds; no interest is drawn for Japan). The euro area's nations after Germany are described on their own pages.

A level's figures are that government's own. Debt one level owes another is in the borrower's figures, but netted out of the total where the total is consolidated, so the levels need not add up to the total, and usually add up to more. The same holds for interest.

## Canada

Federal, Provincial, Municipal and Non-federal (the provinces and municipalities together); none ticked, the default, draws the totals. Debt from 1867 (federal; the others from 1933) and interest from 1926: Historical Statistics of Canada, the national balance sheets and accounts, then Statistics Canada's table 10-10-0015, quarterly, from 1990. The Canada and Quebec Pension Plans are left out.

Each level is joined from its own sources, oldest first, as the total is; the non-federal level is provincial plus municipal within each source before the joins. The Canada and Quebec Pension Plans, which the national accounts count within general government, are left out: their liabilities are pension entitlements, not borrowing.

- **Debt, 1867 to 1960.** Historical Statistics of Canada, second edition ([section H](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-516-x/sectionh/4057752-eng.htm)): federal bonded debt less sinking funds and treasury bills (H37 and H38) from 1867; provincial net funded debt and treasury bills (H384 and H385) and local net funded debt (H400) from 1933.
- **Debt, 1961 to 1989.** The national balance sheets of the federal government ([36-10-0533](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=3610053301)), the provincial governments ([36-10-0535](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=3610053501)) and the local governments ([36-10-0536](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=3610053601)), annual, archived: the short-term paper and bonds each owes, at year end.
- **Debt, 1990 on.** Statistics Canada's government finance statistics, table [10-10-0015](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=1010001501), quarterly, the balance sheet of each level: all its liabilities except equity (special drawing rights, currency and deposits, debt securities, loans, insurance and pension schemes, other accounts payable). Published about three months after each quarter: the release of 2026-09-25 brought the second quarter of 2026.
- **Interest, 1926 to 1960.** The 1968 national accounts' government revenue and expenditure by level ([36-10-0177](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=3610017701) annual, [36-10-0142](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=3610014201) quarterly from 1951), hospitals counted with the provinces. Archived.
- **Interest, 1961 to 1990.** The sector accounts, table [36-10-0245](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=3610024501), federal, provincial and local, quarterly at annual rates. Archived.
- **Interest, 1990 on.** Table 10-10-0015, each level's expense "Interest", quarterly, not annualised: the twelve months are the sum of four quarters.

At each join the older source is scaled to meet the newer one over its last five years. The balance sheets hold bonds and bills alone, the newer table every liability, so the debts are scaled up most at that join: federal by as much as 36 %, provincial 61 %, municipal 80 %, over 1985 to 1989. Elsewhere the scaling is smaller: Historical Statistics' provincial and municipal debts by 12 % over 1956 to 1960, and interest by 7 % at most. In table 10-10-0015 local interest dips to about $480 million a quarter in 2007, against about $850 million in 2006 and $770 million in 2008; it is drawn as published.

## The United States

Federal, State, Local and State & Local; none ticked, the default, draws the totals. Federal debt: the Treasury's, quarterly from 1966; state and local debt together: the Federal Reserve's, from 1945, split by the Census Bureau's yearly counts from 1952. Interest: the national accounts, quarterly from 1947, split between state and local by their annual figures from 1959.

The federal level and the state and local level together are quarterly series; the split between state and local comes from yearly figures, applied to the quarters.

- **Federal debt.** The Treasury's total public debt, [GFDEBTN on FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/GFDEBTN), quarterly from 1966, as on the [debt page](?help=debt).
- **State and local debt together.** The Federal Reserve's [SLGSDODNS on FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SLGSDODNS), debt securities and loans, quarterly from 1945.
- **State and local debt apart.** The state governments' share of the two levels' total debt outstanding at each fiscal year's end, from the Census Bureau's counts: its historical database of government finances for fiscal 1902 to 2008 ([historical data](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/gov-finances/data/historical-data.html)) and its Annual Survey of State and Local Government Finances from 2009 ([the survey](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/gov-finances.html)), each year from 1952. The share is drawn straight between one 30 June and the next and applied to SLGSDODNS, so state plus local always equals it. The program's counts run to the newest fiscal year the Census has published (fiscal 2024 in November 2026), and the program's daily update adds each new year, after a review; after the newest, its share is held, with a warning.
- **Federal interest.** The national accounts' federal interest payments, [A091RC1Q027SBEA on FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/A091RC1Q027SBEA), quarterly at annual rates from 1947.
- **State and local interest together.** [B111RC1Q027SBEA on FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/B111RC1Q027SBEA), quarterly at annual rates from 1947.
- **State and local interest apart.** The national accounts' annual interest payments of state governments ([W756RC1A027NBEA](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/W756RC1A027NBEA)) and local governments ([W856RC1A027NBEA](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/W856RC1A027NBEA)), from 1959 to 2025. Each year's shares are applied to its four quarters, so each year's last twelve months equal the published annual figures, and the quarterly movement is kept.

## The United Kingdom

Central and Local: the UK has no level between them, the devolved governments of Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland being inside central government in its accounts. None ticked, the default, draws the totals. From the ONS: central government's gross debt monthly from 1975, local government's quarterly from 1966, and each one's interest quarterly from 1946, summed over four quarters.

Non-federal, asked of the UK, is its local government.

- **Central government debt.** [BKPW at the ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/governmentpublicsectorandtaxes/publicsectorfinance/timeseries/bkpw/pusf), total central government gross debt, monthly from 1975, millions of pounds, from the public sector finances (the release of 2026-10-21 brought September 2026).
- **Local government debt.** [MDYT at the ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/governmentpublicsectorandtaxes/publicspending/timeseries/mdyt/edp), local government gross debt, quarterly from 1966, from the government finance statistics: the release of 2026-09-22 brought the second quarter of 2026; the next is on 2026-12-22.
- **Central government interest.** [NMFX at the ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/governmentpublicsectorandtaxes/publicsectorfinance/timeseries/nmfx/pusf), "CG: Current expenditure: Net Interest payable", quarterly from 1946.
- **Local government interest.** [NUGW at the ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/governmentpublicsectorandtaxes/publicsectorfinance/timeseries/nugw/pusf), "LG: Current expenditure: Interest", quarterly from 1946.

Interest is summed over four quarters.

## Germany

Federal, Länder, Local and Länder & Local; none ticked, the default, draws the totals. Debt: Eurostat's quarters by subsector from 2000, the Federal Statistical Office's table before, from 1950-03-31 (West Germany's to 1990). Interest: Eurostat's quarters from 2002, the Office's yearly national accounts before, from 1970 (West Germany's to 1990). The social security funds are left out.

- **Debt.** Eurostat's dataset [gov_10q_ggdebt](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/gov_10q_ggdebt/default/table?lang=en), gross debt by subsector, quarterly from 2000: the federal government with its special funds (central government, S.1311), the Länder (state government, S.1312) and local government (S.1313). The social security funds (S.1314) are left out. The levels add up to about 1 % more than the total.
- **Debt before 2000.** The Federal Statistical Office's table behind [West Germany's debt](?help=debt) has each level from 1950-03-31, on the same dates: the Bund (its special funds from 1954), the Länder and the Gemeinden. All of Germany's from 1991. At the end of 2000 Eurostat's federal figure is 5.2 % above the table's, the Länder's 4.9 % and local government's 11.8 %, a difference of definition, so each rises to Eurostat's by a ramp over 1995 to 1999, as the total does before 1991. To 1994 the levels add up to the table's total, not to the total drawn, which is 3.1 % above it at the end of 1991.
- **Interest.** Eurostat's dataset [gov_10q_ggnfa](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/gov_10q_ggnfa/default/table?lang=en), interest payable (D.41) by the same subsectors, quarterly from 2002, summed over four quarters.
- **Interest before 2002.** The Federal Statistical Office's national accounts give each level's interest paid for each year, from the same tables as [the total's](?help=interest): the Bund's (table 3.4.3.4), the Länder's (3.4.3.5) and the Gemeinden's (3.4.3.6), West Germany's from 1970 to 1990, then all of Germany's from 1991 to 2001. Each level's own, as Eurostat's subsectors are, so they add up to a little more than the total. The Office's figures from 1999 are Eurostat's exactly, its year 2002 the sum of Eurostat's four quarters, so the two join with no step; unification is a step at 1991. The older edition that gives the total's interest from 1950 has no level apart, so the levels begin with 1970.

## France

Central and Local: France has no state level, and its social security funds are no level of the chart. None ticked, the default, draws the totals. Eurostat's figures by subsector: debt quarterly from 2000, interest yearly from 1995, each year's at its last day, Eurostat's quarterly interest being for general government alone.

- **Debt.** Eurostat's dataset [gov_10q_ggdebt](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/gov_10q_ggdebt/default/table?lang=en), gross debt by subsector, quarterly from 2000: central government (S.1311) and local government (S.1313). Each subsector's debt is consolidated within it but not with the others. Eurostat has no state level for France (S.1312 is empty); the social security funds (S.1314) are left out.
- **Interest.** Eurostat's yearly accounts, dataset [gov_10a_main](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/gov_10a_main/default/table?lang=en), interest payable (D.41) by the same subsectors, from 1995, each year's drawn at its last day as the twelve months to it. Eurostat's quarterly interest for France is for general government alone (measured 2026-11-20).

## Spain

Each subsector's debt is consolidated within it alone, so the levels add up to more than the total: €1,990 billion against €1,763 billion in 2026-Q2.

## The Netherlands

Eurostat's figures by subsector: debt quarterly from 2000, interest yearly from 1995. Central government's debt alone runs above the total: €582 billion against €524 billion in 2026-Q2.

- **Debt.** Eurostat's dataset [gov_10q_ggdebt](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/gov_10q_ggdebt/default/table?lang=en), gross debt by subsector, quarterly from 2000: central government (S.1311) and local government (S.1313), €53 billion in 2026-Q2. ## Japan

Central and Local, debt alone: no interest is drawn for Japan. The Bank of Japan's flow of funds, each level's loans and debt securities, each March from 1980 to 1997, then each quarter's end from 1997-Q4; none ticked, the default, draws the total. With the social security funds the two add up to the total exactly.

- **Debt.** The Bank of Japan's flow of funds, each sector's loans and debt securities (liabilities), as for the total ([Aggregate public debt](?help=debt)): central government and local governments, at the end of each fiscal year (March) from 1980 to 1997, then at each quarter's end from 1997-Q4. The flow of funds does not consolidate the levels: in 2026-Q2 central government owed ¥1,113.9 trillion and local governments ¥156.8 trillion, and with the social security funds' ¥3.1 trillion they add up to the total, ¥1,273.8 trillion, exactly. Japan has no state level; its social security funds are no level of the chart.
- **No interest.** Japan's interest paid is published yearly, by fiscal year, in the Cabinet Office's national accounts; there are no quarterly figures, by level or in total, so the levels are drawn without it, with a note.

## How the program keeps these figures

Each source's figures are kept in the program as the source published them. A job on GitHub asks every live source each day and adds the new figures; a revision of older ones waits for review before it is taken. When a chart is drawn, the program takes only the newest figures from each source and draws the older ones from what it holds: those from 24 months before the last figure it holds where the source revises its figures (GDP, debt, interest, population and the mortgage rates from surveys), from two months before where it doesn't (the yields, and the rates that are set or traded). If a source doesn't answer, the chart is drawn from what the program holds, with a warning naming the source and its last date. Statistics Canada is checked once a day: on a day it doesn't answer, it is asked nothing more until the next day in Ottawa, and where another source publishes the same figures (the consumer price index at the Bank of Canada, GDP in the IMF's copy at FRED, interest, consolidated and federal, at the IMF itself), that source's newer figures follow Statistics Canada's last, and the warning names them. The sources that have stopped (Statistics Canada's old tables, Historical Statistics of Canada, the Bank of Canada's historical tables, the Bank of England's dataset, and the series the Federal Reserve, the Bundesbank and the ECB no longer update) are kept as they were last published, and asked nothing.
