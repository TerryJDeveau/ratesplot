# Compare the nations

A chart of its own after the nations' charts, under a tab named Compare: one measure, with a curve for each nation ticked, each in its own colour (Canada red, the United States blue, the United Kingdom purple, Germany near-black). Ticking Compare the nations, under Country selection, adds the chart and brings its tab to the front; the nations' charts are left as they are. It is a prototype (2026-11-11): what it draws and how it is laid out are open to change once it has been tried.

The measure is chosen by the boxes of the block Comparison chart, one at a time, under four headings: Bond yields, Real bond yields (inflation-linked), Other measures and Balance of payments. The block is shown while the Compare tab is in front, and there the curves' boxes and the rates on the yield axis are left out, since they do not change this chart; back on a nation's tab they return as they were, and the comparison's block is left out.

- **a yield term**, the 10-year by default: each nation's yield of that term, as its own chart draws it (Canada's monthly history before 2001 in steps, the daily yields after, the day's quotes with the [latest values](?help=latest-values)). A nation without the term is left off, with a note in the message log: the UK has no 2-year yield, Canada no 20-year. The [real yields](?help=yields) are terms too.
- **Policy rate**: each central bank's [policy rate](?help=policy-rates), its target, held in steps from each change; the U.S. target range as a band.
- **Mortgage rate**: each nation's headline [mortgage rate](?help=mortgages), named in the legend, since no term is common to all four: Canada's 5-year conventional (posted) rate, the U.S. 30-year fixed, the UK's 2-year fixed at 75 % loan to value, Germany's fixed for over 5 and up to 10 years; on the left axis, as the policy rate is.
- **Inflation**: each nation's [inflation](?help=inflation), the consumer price index's rise over twelve months.
- **Consumer price index**: each nation's [price index](?help=price-index), not seasonally adjusted, rebased to 100 at the first date in the window where every nation drawn has a value (the axis's label gives it), on a log axis: the curves start together and part by their inflation since.
- **Public debt**, **TTM GDP**, **TTM interest paid on public debt**: each nation's aggregate (general government) [debt](?help=debt), [GDP](?help=gdp) or [interest](?help=interest), in total, not by level of government. [As % of GDP](?help=percent-of-gdp) gives debt and interest as a share of GDP, which needs no currency; GDP, -r's divisor, is then compared as an amount. Otherwise the amounts are in one currency: the [master currency](?help=master-currency) when one is ticked, else the U.S. dollar, each nation's converted at the rate of the End date, which the message log gives. [Per capita](?help=per-capita) and [inflation adjusted](?help=constant-prices) measure them as on the nations' charts, each nation's at its own consumer prices. The [projections](?help=projections) carry each on to today, faint.
- **Population**: each nation's [population](?help=population), in persons, the same under every measure.
- **Current account balance**, **Trade balance**, under the heading Balance of payments: each nation's balance, money in less money out over the trailing year, the pair [Balance of payments](?help=payments) draws on a nation's chart. Measured as the amounts are: in the master currency or US$, a share of GDP under As % of GDP, per person, at constant prices, carried on to today with the [projections](?help=projections) from the earlier of the pair's last figures. On the left axis, linear, with 0 marked, since a balance changes sign; no regression segments.

The dates, the [line labels](?help=end-labels), the [regression segments](?help=regression) (for the amounts and the population), the [daily values](?help=daily) and the [hover read-out](?help=hover) are the ones every chart shares. What a nation's chart chooses for itself under its tab (its terms, its levels, its Min, Max, Top and Bottom) does not reach this chart, so its axes are fitted to its curves; dragging a box on it, or panning it, moves the dates alone. Export chart writes its curves named for their nations ("Canada: 10-Year Yield").

The chart reads what the nations' charts have already downloaded, so it asks no source for anything new.

## Against a base nation

Ticking **Base nation**, in the block under the measure, draws the chart against one nation, chosen by the boxes under it, one at a time: the United States unless another is ticked. Its curve is drawn flat at 0, whether or not it is ticked under Country selection, and each other nation's curve becomes its difference from it, at each date from the base nation's value then in force:

- a measure in percent (a yield, the policy rate, inflation, debt or interest as a share of GDP under [As % of GDP](?help=percent-of-gdp)): the difference in percentage points, "+0.85%" where the nation's is 0.85 point above the base's;
- an amount in a currency, GDP, the population or the price index: the percentage above or below the base's, "+12.3%";
- a balance of payments' balance: its difference from the base's, in the currency ("+$85.2B"), or in percentage points under As % of GDP.

Everything is then drawn on the left axis, its label naming the base, and the title ends with it ("(base: United States)"). The U.S. target range as the base is its midpoint; as another nation's curve, both its limits are made differences. Before the base nation's first value, a curve has nothing to be measured from, and is not drawn there. The [regression segments](?help=regression) are not drawn against a base. The read-out, Export chart and the line labels give the differences.

## TTM % change

Ticking **TTM % change**, first in the block Comparison chart, above the measure's boxes, compares each nation's change over the twelve months to each date in place of the measure itself: its value against its value on the same calendar day a year before (28 February for 29 February), a stepped series' the value then in force:

- a measure in percent (a yield, the policy rate, inflation, debt or interest as a share of GDP under [As % of GDP](?help=percent-of-gdp)): the change in percentage points, "+0.25%" where it rose by a quarter of a point;
- an amount in a currency, GDP, the population or the price index: the change in per cent, "+3.4%", and nothing where the year-ago value is not above zero (the price index's change is each nation's inflation);
- a balance of payments' balance: the change in its currency, now less a year before, "+$85.2B", so that a narrowing deficit reads as a rise (a percentage of a year-ago balance near 0 would run to thousands); in percentage points under As % of GDP.

Each nation is read from a year before the Start date, so the window's first year has its change. Everything is drawn on the left axis, labelled "TTM change (percentage points)" or "TTM % change", and the title ends ", TTM Change" or ", TTM % Change". The U.S. target range is its midpoint. A stretch carried on to today with the [projections](?help=projections) stays faint, its line label marked "≈". With a base nation, each nation's change less the base's is drawn, in percentage points. The [regression segments](?help=regression) are not drawn on the changes. The read-out, Export chart and the line labels give the changes.

## On the command line

`--compare`, at least `--com`, draws the 10-year yields compared; `--no-compare` takes the chart off. `--compare:MEASURE` chooses the measure and turns the chart on: a yield term as `--yields` writes it (`--compare:2y`, `--compare:r10`; `--compare:10` is the 10-year), or `policy`, `mortgage`, `inflation`, `cpi`, `debt`, `gdp`, `interest`, `population`, `current` or `trade`, each spelled by a leading part (`--com:pol`, `--com:pop`, `--com:cu`). One measure at a time: `--compare:10y,2y` is refused. `--base` draws it against the United States, `--base:NATION` against another by its code as `--nations` takes it (`--base:gb`, at least `--bas:`), one at a time; `--no-base` draws it without one. `--ttm` compares the TTM changes; `--no-ttm` the measure itself. The nations compared are those ticked (`--nations:ca,us,gb,de`). With `--save` the chart has a file of its own, `_compare` added to the name, or a sheet named Compare in a workbook: [Command line](?help=command-line).
